# Comparing Methods for Building Reliable Prediction Intervals for Machine-Learning Inflation Forecasts in Commodity-Dependent Economies

Short version for a workshop on conformal prediction and forecasting, or for arXiv. Draft 2026-11-19. Full text: `main.md`. Notes for the reviewer: `REVIEW_NOTES.md`.

---

## Abstract

Prediction intervals for inflation are used for decisions in commodity-dependent economies, but it is not known whether adaptive conformal methods that perform well on euro-area and US data keep their nominal coverage there. We build a multi-horizon (1, 3, 6 and 12 months) calibration benchmark of split, rolling-window, adaptive (ACI, FACI), conformal PID and regime-aware (DRACP) intervals at nominal level 0.90, using monthly IMF consumer price indices for 13 design economies, 7 hold-out economies and, in an extension, a second hold-out panel of 10 economies, the hold-out panels selected by fixed data-availability rules. The primary outcome is the calibration error (CE), the mean over countries of |coverage - 0.90|. Contrasts are tested with a moving-block bootstrap and Holm correction, equivalence of DRACP and ACI with two one-sided tests and a 2 percentage-point margin, and decision rules R1-R5 were pre-specified in a version-controlled protocol before the hold-out run (a self-attested commit history; the rules were written after the exploratory results were known). Adaptive methods were better calibrated than a calibration performed once and than the native intervals of the forecasting model, with a consistent direction. The registered rule R1 was met only at a 96-month warm-up, a sensitivity run that is not promoted, and not at the primary 48-month warm-up (ACI minus split at h=3: -3.0 pp, p=0.054; at h=6: -2.8 pp, p=0.092; Holm-adjusted 0.108 at both horizons). On the design and first hold-out panels an advantage of ACI over a rolling window was not shown (Holm p 0.35 and 1.0 on the first hold-out panel), no efficiency advantage is established, and DRACP against ACI is inconclusive; because the 2 pp equivalence margin is about the size of ACI's own calibration error, the equivalence verdict is weak by construction. At h=12 pooled coverage is below nominal for every method, and releasing outcomes immediately raised ACI coverage by 2.4 pp (95% CI -0.1 to 4.8), below the pre-specified 3 pp threshold. In a secondary, post hoc analysis without multiplicity correction, ACI with an ARIMA base model had a lower calibration error than the native ARIMA intervals at every horizon in both samples (-3.3 to -6.4 pp). An extension, specified before any forecast was computed for its countries, adds a second hold-out panel of 10 economies and a zero-shot foundation model (Chronos). On the second panel ACI improved on a rolling window under the registered rule at warm-up 48 at h=3 for all four base models and at h=6 for ARIMA and naive B, while the earlier 20 countries re-run from 2010-01 again show it as not met: the conclusion depends on the panel, the reason is not known, the panels are not pooled, and warm-up 96 on the new panel (41 and 35 pooled months) shows nothing. DRACP against ACI stays inconclusive. On target months from 2025-01 Chronos was descriptively at least as accurate as LightGBM and ARIMA, under an unverified assumption about its pretraining cutoff. The hold-out countries are untouched only with respect to countries.

## 1 Introduction

A prediction interval with a nominal level of 90 percent claims that about nine realised values in ten fall inside it. Whether this holds is a question of calibration that can be checked against realised data; it is not guaranteed by the forecasting model, whose error distribution is estimated from a past that may not resemble the future. Commodity-dependent economies are a demanding setting, because their inflation regimes can change within a few years [CITATION NEEDED: empirical evidence on the volatility and regime changes of inflation in commodity-dependent economies].

Conformal prediction (Vovk, Gammerman and Shafer, 2005) gives finite-sample coverage under exchangeability, which time series violate. Adaptive conformal methods (Gibbs and Candes, 2021, 2024; Angelopoulos, Candes and Tibshirani, 2023; Bhatnagar et al., 2023) were designed for that case, and a recent regime-aware variant, DRACP (Oancea, 2026), reports gains on euro-area inflation. Whether such results transfer to commodity-dependent economies and to multi-month horizons has not been tested to our knowledge [CITATION NEEDED: no systematic literature search was run].

This is a transfer and benchmark study and not a new method. We evaluate six interval methods on top of point forecasts of the cumulative h-month log growth of the consumer price index, with outcome feedback delayed by h months as in real use. The panel has 13 economies used to design the study and 7 hold-out economies for which no forecast existed before the pre-specified hold-out run. We report outcomes exactly as the pre-specified rules classify them; most are null or inconclusive, and a null outcome is not evidence of equality. An extension adds a second hold-out panel of 10 economies and a zero-shot foundation model as a fourth base forecaster (Sections 3 to 5).

Contributions: (1) a calibration benchmark on 20 economies; (2) evidence that adaptive methods are better calibrated than a calibration performed once and than native model intervals, with a consistent direction in both samples and with the registered rule R1 met only at the sensitivity warm-up on the first hold-out panel (and at the primary warm-up on the second); (3) a feedback-delay diagnostic; (4) an extension showing that the answer to "does ACI improve on a rolling window" depends on the panel, reported and not resolved, and a descriptive accuracy comparison of Chronos on months it cannot be assumed to have seen; (5) an open pipeline with hashed data snapshots, tests and a committed protocol.

## 2 Related work

Conformal prediction gives finite-sample coverage under exchangeability (Vovk, Gammerman and Shafer, 2005). Beyond it, Barber et al. (2023) bound the coverage gap, Xu and Xie (2021) build intervals for dynamic time series, and Zaffran et al. (2022) study adaptive conformal predictions. ACI (Gibbs and Candes, 2021) updates the working miscoverage level online by a step size times the difference between the target and the realised miss indicator. FACI (Gibbs and Candes, 2024) aggregates several step sizes, conformal PID control (Angelopoulos, Candes and Tibshirani, 2023) combines proportional, integral and forecasting components, and SAOCP (Bhatnagar et al., 2023) uses strongly adaptive online learning. These guarantees concern long-run miss frequency and are not stated for outcomes that arrive h months late. Chronos (Ansari et al., 2024) is a family of pretrained forecasting models that can be used without fitting.

DRACP, as described by its authors (Oancea, 2026, arXiv:2608.17079v2), keeps the split-conformal radius but replaces the equal weights on past errors by a weighted quantile whose weight combines temporal decay, a density-ratio correction, a Gaussian kernel and a regime-similarity term, with an online controller of the significance level. It is evaluated on 48 macroeconomic and financial series (28 HICP inflation series, 13 US macro and energy series, 7 daily financial series), mainly at one step, with an additional multi-horizon evaluation (h=3, 6, 12; delayed feedback). The authors state that the advantage is specific to one-step-ahead forecasting and depends on the point forecaster [VERIFY against the paper]; our study does not test that claim. We cite no literature on the calibration of inflation intervals in emerging markets [CITATION NEEDED].

## 3 Data and setting

Monthly consumer price indices (all items) are from the IMF (series `M.<country>.PCPI_IX`); the exchange rate (domestic currency per US dollar, IMF IFS) and the Brent price (IMF primary commodity prices) are features of the LightGBM forecaster. The target is y(t, h) = 100 ln(CPI(t+h) / CPI(t)) for horizons h in {1, 3, 6, 12}, which is invariant to the index base.

**Design countries (13):** BR, MX, CL, CO, PE, ZA, ID, KZ, RU, AZ, UZ, NG, MN; they were used to shape the design (contrasts, horizons, warm-up). **Hold-out countries (7):** BO, PY, UY, GH, GE, KG, AO. They were fixed by a written data-availability rule (at least 220 months of IMF monthly CPI and exchange rate within 2000-01 to 2026-06, at most 3 missing CPI months, in a fixed candidate order); 7 of 14 candidates qualified and the rule was not relaxed. Target months of the hold-out panel run from 2009-02 to 2026-06. **Second hold-out panel (extension, 10):** ZM, AM, TZ, UG, KE, IQ, BW, NA, MZ, CI, the first 10 eligible of a candidate list fixed in `fetch_holdout2.mjs` before any forecast of these countries was computed (at least 170 months of IMF CPI and exchange rate within 2010-01 to 2026-06, at most 3 missing CPI months); 198 months from 2010-01, SHA-256 `72bcfee645ef3c5f89a67763278a3ee8ee2a74523e38a26739b16726c8947c75`. After the 60-month training minimum and warm-up 48 it has 89 pooled months at h=3 and 83 at h=6 (41 and 35 at warm-up 96). No export-share criterion for "commodity-dependent" was applied, and Georgia and Uruguay are borderline. Snapshots are dated and hashed (SHA-256). Nigeria's series ends at 2024-12 because of a methodology break, and Russia enters only through stretches of at least 60 consecutive months.

## 4 Methods

**Point forecasts and intervals.** Forecasts are made at one origin per month on an expanding window using only data dated at or before the origin. The primary base model is LightGBM (Ke et al., 2017), a direct forecast with fixed hyperparameters; ARIMA and naive B serve as other base models. Six methods are run on the same point forecasts through the released `dracp` package: split conformal (quantile of absolute residuals on the warm-up, never updated); rolling conformal (most recent 120 residuals); ACI (step 0.02, window 120); FACI and conformal PID with package defaults; and DRACP with the configuration of the reproduction script. The nominal level is 0.90. The primary warm-up is 48 residuals; a warm-up of 96 is a sensitivity run applied to all methods and is not promoted to the primary result. **Chronos (extension).** A fourth base model, `amazon/chronos-bolt-tiny`, used zero-shot on the monthly inflation series; the h median forecasts are summed, it has no native interval, and the conformal methods are applied on its point forecast. It may have seen public CPI series in pretraining, so target months before 2025-01 are flagged `possibly_seen`; the cutoff was not verified and 2025-01 is a conservative assumption.

**Delayed feedback.** For an h-step forecast made at origin j, the outcome is observed at month j+h. At origin k the update for origin j is applied only if j+h <= k, and the interval actually issued is the one scored. A diagnostic option releases each outcome at origin + 1; it is not a valid forecasting setup and isolates the effect of delay.

**Calibration error.** For unit u and method m, CE(u, m) = |cov(u, m) - 0.90|, and a contrast between methods m1 and m2 is T = mean over units of [CE(u, m1) - CE(u, m2)]; a negative T means m1 is better calibrated. CE is used instead of pooled coverage because opposite deviations of different countries cancel in a pooled average. The interval (Winkler, 1972) score is W = (u - l) + (2/alpha) max(l - y, 0) + (2/alpha) max(y - u, 0), with alpha = 0.10; lower is better (see also Gneiting and Raftery, 2007).

**Inference.** Origin calendar months are resampled in moving blocks (Kunsch, 1989) of length max(h, 12), jointly across units, with 2000 replicates; we report the estimate, a 95 percent percentile interval and a two-sided centred bootstrap p-value. The primary family has four tests (ACI minus rolling and DRACP minus ACI, each at h=3 and h=6, LightGBM base), controlled by Holm's procedure (Holm, 1979). ACI minus split is a secondary contrast outside the family. DRACP against ACI is also tested for equivalence with two one-sided tests (Schuirmann, 1987) and a margin of 2 pp of CE; equivalence is declared if the larger one-sided p-value is below 0.05, otherwise the verdict is "not shown". The minimum detectable difference (MDE) is simulated from the observed hit pattern of the baseline; a contrast with an undefined MDE or an MDE above 7 pp is inconclusive (rule R5).

**Pre-specified rules.** R1: ACI is better calibrated than split if ACI minus split is negative with Holm p < 0.05 at both h=3 and h=6. R2: "ACI improves on rolling" only if ACI minus rolling is negative with Holm p < 0.05, otherwise "not shown". R3: "no advantage, equivalent within 2 pp" only if equivalence holds at both warm-ups; "DRACP better" only if DRACP minus ACI is negative with Holm p < 0.05 at both; otherwise "inconclusive". R4: delayed feedback explains the h=12 shortfall if ACI coverage with instant feedback exceeds delayed by at least 3 pp on the hold-out panel. R5 as above. The rules were committed before the hold-out run, in a self-attested commit history that is not an external registry or an independent timestamp, and they were written after the exploratory results were known.

**Extension rules.** The eighth protocol entry (commit `dfe0b2c`) fixed the second panel, Chronos and the rules E1 to E5 before any forecast was computed for the new countries; the code is at `ca43ddc` and the results at `accd674`. The rules are applied per base model with the same family of four tests (Holm within a base model, not across the four base models, which share countries and months). E1: R1, R2 or R3 is "replicated" only if the same rule is met in the same direction. E2: the four base models side by side. E3 and E4: Chronos accuracy is judged only on target months from 2025-01. E5: a contrast with an undefined minimum detectable difference, or one above 7 pp, is inconclusive. Extension runs start in 2010-01, so the re-run of the earlier 20 countries differs from the main runs that start in 2000-01. A language-model explainer is implemented and tested with a deterministic fake client; its 468-prompt evaluation has not been run on a real model and no result is reported.

## 5 Results

All results use LightGBM and level 0.90 unless stated otherwise. Unadjusted p-values outside the registered family are descriptive; the word "significant" is not used for them.

**Pooled coverage.** On the hold-out panel at warm-up 48, split conformal over-covers at h=1, 3 and 6 (0.954, 0.932, 0.936), while ACI is at 0.914, 0.907 and 0.908 and DRACP at 0.917, 0.904 and 0.894. At h=12 pooled coverage is below nominal for every method (0.833 to 0.884), with wide pooled bootstrap intervals that for ACI, DRACP, rolling and split do not exclude 0.90.

**Rule outcomes (first hold-out panel).** R1 was not met at the primary warm-up: ACI minus split was -3.0 pp (95% CI [-5.4, +0.5], p 0.054) at h=3 and -2.8 pp ([-3.9, +1.2], p 0.092) at h=6, Holm-adjusted 0.108 at both horizons. It was met at warm-up 96 (-4.1 pp, p 0.018, and -5.9 pp, p 0.0025; Holm 0.018 and 0.005), a sensitivity run that is not promoted. The direction replicates in the exploratory data (-4.6 pp at h=3 and -3.4 pp at h=6 at warm-up 48), for three base models, and after removing any single country (no sign flips). The statement supported is therefore one of consistent direction and not of pre-specified confirmation, and at the primary warm-up the confidence intervals include zero. ACI minus rolling was -1.6 pp (95% CI [-3.0, +0.5], Holm p 0.35) at h=3 and -0.7 pp ([-2.8, +1.3], Holm p 1.0) at h=6: not shown. On the Winkler score ACI was 1.0 percent lower than rolling at h=3 (95% CI [-7.1, +4.1]) and 5.3 percent higher at h=6 (descriptive). DRACP minus ACI was -0.1 pp and -0.3 pp at warm-up 48, with equivalence within 2 pp shown (TOST p 0.0005 and 0.047) and not shown at warm-up 96 (0.062 and 0.355). The minimum detectable difference is undefined on the full hold-out sample, so all four primary contrasts are inconclusive (R5). R2 was not shown (Holm p 0.35 and 1.0 at warm-up 48; 0.59 and 0.99 at 96), R3 is inconclusive, and R4 was not met (see below).

**Width and efficiency.** Three statistics should be kept apart. At h=1 and h=3, ACI, DRACP and FACI have a median interval width below that of rolling in both samples (ratio for ACI 0.91 and 0.93 exploratory, 0.84 and 0.90 hold-out). The pooled mean width of ACI at h=3 is larger than rolling's in both samples (4.28 against 3.95; 5.856 against 5.786). The Winkler ratio to rolling at h=1 and h=3 is 0.94 to 0.99 on the hold-out panel and 0.99 to 1.16 in the exploratory data. No efficiency advantage is established.

**Native intervals (post hoc, secondary).** ACI with an ARIMA base model is compared with the native ARIMA intervals. The analysis was written into the protocol before it was computed but is post hoc; p-values are unadjusted and there is no multiplicity correction. ACI had a lower CE than the native ARIMA intervals at every horizon in both samples, by 3.3 to 6.4 pp (at h=3, CE 0.066 against 0.015 exploratory and 0.058 against 0.013 hold-out); on the hold-out panel the interval excludes zero at h=1 and h=3 and includes it at h=6 ([-4.4, +1.2]) and h=12 ([-4.9, +1.3]).
**Delayed feedback at h=12 (S3).** The change in h=12 coverage when outcomes are released instantly is +4.1 [1.4, 7.8] pp for ACI in the exploratory data and +2.4 [-0.1, 4.8] on the hold-out panel (paired block-bootstrap 95% interval; DRACP +4.3 and +3.9 pp, FACI +8.3 and +6.4 pp). R4 was not met for ACI: coverage was 0.884 with delayed and 0.908 with instant feedback on the hold-out panel. Delay accounts for part of the shortfall, with a size that depends on the method and the sample; it is not shown that delay is, or is not, the main cause. The diagnostic is not a valid forecasting setup.

The conclusion about ACI against a rolling window therefore depends on the panel. The data do not say why; a shorter series, more volatile and lower-income economies, a warm-up that takes a larger share of 198 months, and a rolling window with a larger CE here (0.066 to 0.090 at h=3 against 0.036 on the first hold-out panel) are candidate explanations that were not tested. At warm-up 48 the minimum detectable difference for ACI minus rolling is 1.2 to 6.6 pp (undefined for Chronos at h=3, not conclusive under E5); where R2 is not met at h=6 it is 6.1 and 6.6 pp, so "not met" there is not evidence of absence. **R3 stays inconclusive:** DRACP has a lower CE than ACI in every cell by 0.5 to 1.8 pp, none at Holm p below 0.05; equivalence within 2 pp holds only for Chronos at h=3 (TOST p 0.0385), and every DRACP minus ACI contrast has an undefined minimum detectable difference.

The stress analysis against exchange-rate shocks is secondary and exploratory, and the exchange-rate flag is a weak proxy of inflation shocks.

## 6 Discussion and limitations

**What is supported.** Adaptive methods (ACI, FACI, conformal PID, DRACP) are better calibrated than a calibration performed once and than native model intervals, with a direction that is consistent across both samples, three base models and the removal of any single country. The registered rule R1 was met only at the sensitivity warm-up on the first hold-out panel and at the primary warm-up (not at 96) on the second, and the evidence against native intervals is secondary and post hoc. At h=12 pooled coverage is below nominal for every method, and delayed feedback explains only part of it.

**What is not supported, and what depends on the panel.** Whether ACI improves on a rolling window depends on the panel: not shown on the design panel and the first hold-out panel, not met on the earlier 20 countries re-run from 2010-01, met at warm-up 48 on the second hold-out panel (h=3 for four base models, h=6 for ARIMA and naive B). The reason is not known and candidate explanations were not tested. We neither average the panels nor select the favourable one, and a reader cannot take a general recommendation between the two from this study. The data also do not show that DRACP is better than ACI (lower CE by 0.5 to 1.8 pp on the second panel, none at Holm p below 0.05), or that DRACP fails to transfer. None of the null outcomes is evidence of equality.

**The ceiling effect.** The calibration error of ACI on the first panels is already small (0.013 to 0.020 at h=1 and h=3), about the size of the 2 pp equivalence margin. The equivalence verdict for DRACP against ACI is therefore weak by construction, and the contrasts against ACI cannot be powered there: the simulated alternative cannot reduce CE below zero, so the improvement it can create is bounded by the baseline CE, and the MDE is undefined for all four primary contrasts. Null outcomes were likely before the run. The informative contrasts are those against weak references, split and native intervals. We read this as a limit of the design and not as a finding about the methods. On the second panel the rolling window has a larger CE and the minimum detectable difference for ACI minus rolling is defined, which is consistent with this argument but does not test it. DRACP trains its regime and scale models on half of the warm-up, and its authors report that its advantage is specific to one-step forecasts, which this design does not test.

**The procedural boundary.** The hold-out countries (both panels) are independent of the design sample by country only. They share calendar months with it, the methods, contrasts and horizons were chosen on the design countries, and the author had seen the post-2025-06 months of the design countries before the amendments. The rules R1-R5 and E1-E5 were written after earlier results were known, and the pre-specification is a self-attested commit history. The hold-out results are therefore tests on new countries and not blind tests of the design. The run on the post-2025-06 months of the design countries is degenerate and is not used.

**Other limitations.** The hold-out panel has 7 countries, two of which (Ghana and Angola) have high-inflation episodes. "Commodity-dependent" is not defined by a measurable criterion, and the hold-out set was determined by data availability. LightGBM carries the registered contrasts; ARIMA and naive B give the same direction but were not part of the decision rules. The size of the CE bootstrap at the boundary of the absolute value was not checked by simulation. CPI vintages were not checked. Our reproduction of the one-step DRACP setting was partial (5 countries, one seed). The S6 and S7 additions are post hoc and uncorrected for multiplicity. Point forecasts were simple and not optimised. For the extension: the second panel is short (198 months), its rules were written with all earlier results known, the Holm correction does not extend across base models that share countries and months, only the smallest Chronos-Bolt model was used zero-shot, its cutoff is unverified and its clean sample is 22 months, and ARIMA and naive B were not re-run on the earlier 20 countries.

## 7 Reproducibility

The code, the tests, the protocol with its dated change log, the dated and hashed data snapshots and the result tables are in the project repository (`docs/thesis/` and `scripts/thesis/exp/`, branch `thesis/inflation-calibration`); the commit history of the pre-specification is described in `main.md` Section 5. The engine needs a Python 3.12 environment with the `dracp` package (0.2.0 in the reproduction check) and runs with `-m exp.run`; the tests are run with `-m pytest exp/tests -q` (81 tests; the language-model layer has 31 more). Commits: specification of the extension `dfe0b2c`, code `ca43ddc`, results `accd674`; the extension used `amazon/chronos-bolt-tiny` and the data file `holdout2.json` with the hash given in Section 3. Implementation note: the country code NA (Namibia) was first read as a missing value by the CSV reader; the reader was fixed, the forecasts were not affected.
